Genealogy update · August 17, 2026

Plymouth changed the trail

After spending a week in Plymouth, Massachusetts, walking many of the same places connected to my ancestors, I came home wanting to do more than keep names on a family tree. I wanted to document the connections and prove them.

That decision started the process of applying to the Pilgrim John Howland Society through my descent from Mayflower passengers John Howland and Elizabeth Tilley. I have also submitted my lineage to the Connecticut Society of Mayflower Descendants as the first step toward membership in the General Society of Mayflower Descendants.

The research is already taking us through old town records, vital certificates, census pages, cemetery records, newspapers, probate files, family binders, and the occasional genealogy mystery that refuses to cooperate.

The rule around here

Proof before pedigree

A family tree is a roadmap, not the finish line. When records disagree, the disagreement stays in the notes until better evidence settles it. The goal is not to force the story to fit the tree. The goal is to find out what really happened.

Living family members are intentionally left out of the public lineage below. Their privacy matters more than filling another box on a chart.
